# Store assets: what Play requires, and what is ready

Specs checked against Play Console requirements as of **30 July 2026**.

Everything here is generated by two scripts from the app's own artwork and from
the app actually running, so it can be rebuilt after a design or copy change:

```bash
python store/make-assets.py            # icon + feature graphic
python store/capture-screenshots.py    # helpers used by the capture flow
python store/finish-screenshots.py     # convert raw captures to Play formats
```

## Optional, and done anyway

| Slot | Spec | File | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **10-inch tablet** | up to 8, 1800×2560 ideal | `assets/play/tablet10/` | ✅ 3 files |
| **7-inch tablet** | up to 8, 1200×1920 ideal | `assets/play/tablet7/` | ✅ 3 files |

Tablet screenshots are **not required** to publish. They are here because Play
warns about their absence and may exclude the app from tablet recommendations.
They are real captures at those display sizes, not upscaled phone shots: the
emulator display was resized so the app genuinely relayouted. Play rejects
stretched phone captures.

## Optional, and not done: the promo video

**A promo video is optional and I cannot produce one.** It is not a file upload —
Play takes a **YouTube URL**, so it needs a YouTube account and a published
video. The rules, so you do not waste a take:

- a plain YouTube video URL, not a playlist or channel link
- must not be age-restricted
- monetisation **off**, or ads may play over your store listing
- a feature graphic is required before a video can be shown, which you now have
- it replaces the feature graphic as the first thing people see, so a bad video
  is worse than none

If you do make one, 20–30 seconds is plenty, and the honest shot list is the
product loop, not a montage: buddy hands out a mission → child does it away from
the phone → parent taps approve → the ice cream promise fills up. Screen-record
it on a real phone; the emulator's frame rate looks wrong on video.

## The eight phone screenshots, in upload order

Order matters. Play shows the first two in search results, and most people never
scroll further.

1. `01-today-mission` — a real mission, matched to the interests chosen at setup
2. `02-goal` — the child sees the reward a parent promised, and how far off it is
3. `03-rewards` — the parent screen where that promise is written down
4. `04-mission` — the mission runner with its timer
5. `05-journey` — progress, level and the map
6. `06-buddy` — twelve buddies to choose from
7. `07-parent` — the parent area
8. `08-welcome` — what the app is for, in three lines

### One caveat on number 7

It was captured on a fresh install, so every counter reads zero. Honest, but a
weak advert for the parent side. Re-take it after a week of real use:

```bash
python store/capture-screenshots.py
```

then re-run `finish-screenshots.py`. Do **not** hand-edit numbers into it. Play's
policy on misleading store assets is not worth the risk, and fabricated stats in
a Families app is exactly the sort of thing that draws a manual review.

## Screenshots are of the real app

Every capture came from the release APK running on a Pixel 5 emulator, walked
through setup and the mission flow the way a person would. The status bar was put
into demo mode for a clean clock and full battery, which is cosmetic and normal
practice — nothing about the app itself was staged, and the mission shown is one
the engine actually picked for the interests selected during that run.
